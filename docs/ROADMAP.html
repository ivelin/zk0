zk0 · Public Roadmap

Start slow. Compound.

zk0 is building a way for robot operators to train one shared model together, while each operator keeps their own data on their own machines. This public roadmap shows the direction and the order of work — at a high level, on purpose. It starts with tangible early wins, and speeds up only as real traction earns it.

What zk0 offers: replicated benchmark evaluation — the same tests, run the same way, so results can be checked and compared — and auditable records of what was evaluated and when. That claim is deliberately narrow: evaluation shows how a model performs on the benchmarks tested. It is not a guarantee of performance in every setting, and it is not a certification of anyone's data or contributions.
The short version — easy to pass along

Many robot teams each have a little private data — too little to train a great model alone. zk0 lets them train one shared model together without handing their data to a central pool. Every model version is checked with replicated benchmark evaluation, and the results are kept in auditable records anyone in the network can inspect.

  • Early wins first. Prove the shared model on shared benchmarks, with a small group of committed operators.
  • Trust grows with evidence. Standing in the network is earned slowly, through results that others can re-check — never bought, never assumed.
  • Acceleration is earned. Bigger steps — paid tiers, wider access, stronger accountability — arrive only when usage and results justify them.
01

Two ways to grow

The forced path demands fast, big early wins — and pays for them with a plateau. The zk0 path accepts a deliberately flat start, then accelerates as trust and model quality compound. Tap a legend item to isolate a curve.

zk0 path — acceleration is engineered A small retained core and one clear question to answer first. Then the loop compounds: a better shared model draws more contributors, whose more varied experience makes a better model. Each phase makes the next one cheaper and safer to attempt.
Forced path — the spike that caps growth A big early push promises results before there's evidence behind them. When the promised results don't hold up, trust drains and growth flattens — no matter how much was spent getting there.
02

The engine: a compounding loop, not a launch

Growth doesn't come from a bigger day one. It comes from a loop that gets stronger every time it turns — and from a stock of trust, built on checkable results, that accumulates alongside the model.

Plan on the committed core, not the signups The planning number is a small core of operators who stay and keep contributing. Projections are built on them — signups and registrations are vanity numbers.
Traction unlocks the next step Each phase below begins when a signal appears — operators retained, groups forming, real demand — not when a date arrives. Steps taken early are friction; taken late, they're overdue.
Trust compounds like the model does Records start accruing from the first round, long before standing in the network is worth anything. By the time standing matters, there's a long, inspectable history behind it.

Four phases, in order, as the stakes grow

This is the public shape of the plan. The detailed research, designs, and implementation work behind each phase stay in zk0's private systems and are shared in depth with network members as they join. What is public is the sequence — and the rule that no phase starts before the one before it has earned it.

04

Trust: earned slowly, lost quickly

Standing in the network is deliberately asymmetric. It builds slowly, from benchmark results and records that anyone in the network can re-check — and it can be lost quickly when there is clear evidence of misconduct. That shape is what makes standing hard to fake and costly to abuse.

Why the asymmetry works

Anyone can create a fresh account — but nobody can fake a long history of results that others have already re-checked. Slow accrual makes standing expensive to fake; quick loss makes it irrational to burn on one act of misconduct.

What the records do — and don't — say

The records show what was evaluated, on which benchmarks, and what the results were. They do not certify that any operator's data is genuine, complete, or safe, and benchmark results do not guarantee how a model will perform on different robots, tasks, or environments.

The honest limit

Evaluation and record-keeping can prove statements about a training run — what was submitted, when, and how the resulting model scored. They cannot by themselves prove where data came from or that it came from a real robot. zk0 does not claim otherwise.

That is why the public claim stays narrow: replicated benchmark evaluation and auditable records — a process you can inspect, not a promise you have to take on faith.

P—1

Slow is a strategy, not a delay

Tangible wins first, bigger steps as traction earns them. A flat start with a retained core beats a spike that promises big early results and caps everything after.

P—2

Claims stay as narrow as the evidence

zk0 publishes what its evaluation actually shows — benchmark results and auditable records — and nothing broader. If a claim can't be re-checked, it doesn't go on this page.

P—3

Acceleration is engineered

Trust and model quality compound; launch spikes don't. Every phase is unlocked by a traction signal, and each one makes the next cheaper to run.
